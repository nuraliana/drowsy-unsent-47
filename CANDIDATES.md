# Candidates — Unsent: The Micro-Confession Sprint

Scoring (5 pts each, 25 total): **C1** genuinely unsent (feels like a real
draft, not written to perform) · **C2** specific and honest (concrete detail
only this person would know) · **C3** felt reason it stayed in drafts (the
reader feels WHY it was never sent) · **C4** To: line and message need each
other (neither works alone) · **C5** fresh angle (nothing like the saturated
field: Dad/Mum/brother/voicemail/landlord-key/childhood-home/nurse/grandma,
the Christmas place setting, "got fired in March", the brother's room).

## The candidates

### 1. CHOSEN — To: my ex's mother — 25/25

> I still make your soup when I'm ill, the one you wrote on the back of the
> electricity bill, and I've never told you because saying so would mean
> admitting I kept the best part of a life I walked away from.

Why it scores full marks: the soup-on-the-electricity-bill detail is concrete
and belongs to exactly one person (C2); the ritual (still making it when ill)
is a living, maintained secret, and the reason it stays unsent is stated as a
cost — admitting it would mean admitting she kept the best part of a life she
left (C3); the message is meaningless without the To: line, and the To: line
is the only person who could absolve it (C4); no ex's-mother angle appears in
the 356-entry field (C5). It also reads as a draft, not a performance (C1).

### 2. To: the vet who put Biscuit down — 24/25 (C4: 4)

> I told everyone I held him while you gave the injection, but I was in the
> car park the whole time because I couldn't watch him go, and I've let you
> carry that kindness for three years.

Strong and honest, but the vet already knows she was in the car park — the
confession is really owed to everyone else she lied to, so the To: line and
the message don't fully need each other.

### 3. To: my daughter's swimming coach — 24/25 (C3: 4)

> I told you she quit because of school, but she quit because I couldn't
> afford the fees after the second term, and I let you think she lost
> interest because I was too proud to ask about the hardship place.

Fresh class/shame angle, but the "why it stayed unsent" (pride) is stated
rather than felt in a moment of almost-sending, so C3 is a 4, not a 5.

### 4. To: the shopkeeper who gave me credit — 24/25 (C2: 4)

> I still owe you for the bread and milk from the winter I lost my job, and
> I cross the street when I see you because paying you back would mean
> admitting how close we came to having nothing.

The crossing-the-street avoidance is a felt reason, but "bread and milk" is
a generic detail next to the field's best lines, so C2 is a 4.

### 5. To: my best friend — 24/25 (C5: 4)

> I stopped ringing after your second round of chemo because I didn't know
> what to say, and I let you think I'd moved on instead of admitting I was
> scared of saying the wrong thing to someone who was dying.

Genuinely felt, but "To: my best friend" is one of the most crowded To: lines
in the field, so the angle is not fresh even though the detail is.

### 6. To: the librarian who kept my fines — 24/25 (C3: 4)

> I never returned the atlas I took in Year 9 because it was the only book
> with a map of the town I was leaving, and I let you write it off as lost
> because I couldn't admit I'd already decided to go.

Specific and fresh, but the reason it stayed unsent is slightly abstract —
"couldn't admit I'd decided to go" — rather than a felt, lived cost.

### 7. To: the referee who sent me off in the cup final — 23/25 (C1: 4, C3: 4)

> I told the lads you got the call wrong, but I did handle the ball, and I've
> let you be the villain of that story for fifteen years because admitting it
> would mean the cup was ours to lose.

"Villain of that story" tips toward performance rather than a real draft, and
the reason (pride over a lost cup) carries less emotional weight than the
field's best lines.

### 8. To: the midwife who delivered my daughter — 23/25 (C4: 4, C5: 4)

> I told everyone I asked for the epidural, but I never asked, I just couldn't
> say the words when the pain came, and I've let my husband believe I chose
> it because it was easier than admitting I froze.

The person deceived is the husband, not the midwife, so the message doesn't
quite need its To: line; birth-confession angles also sit close to the
field's hospital/family cluster.
